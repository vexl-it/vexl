# Do's & Don'ts

Concrete writing rules with examples. When in doubt, the [voice pillars](./voice-and-personality.md) win; this file makes them operational.

---

## 1. Talk like a person, not a system

**Do** use "you" and "we". Address the reader directly. Use contractions (you're, we'll, it's, don't).

**Don't** use passive voice to dodge responsibility, or third-person system-speak.

- ✅ "We couldn't create the invoice. Try again in a moment."
- ❌ "An error occurred during invoice generation. Please retry the operation."
- ✅ "We'll let you know when something happens."
- ❌ "Users will be notified upon relevant events."

---

## 2. Hand over control; never gatekeep

**Do** frame actions as the user's choice and keep them in charge.

**Don't** use approval/permission/verification language about people. Vexl approves no one.

- ✅ "Your identity stays hidden until you choose to reveal it."
- ❌ "Your identity will be disclosed once verification is approved."
- ✅ "You decide what to share, and when."
- ❌ "Sharing is permitted after account validation."

---

## 3. Privacy = a calm, practical choice

**Do** present privacy as sensible optionality.

**Don't** sound paranoid, conspiratorial, or preachy. No fear-mongering.

- ✅ "Your number stays private unless you choose to share it."
- ✅ "You don't need privacy until you suddenly do."
- ❌ "They're watching everything you do. Hide before it's too late."

---

## 4. Be clear before clever

**Do** be witty when the moment allows — onboarding, empty states, marketing.

**Don't** let a joke obscure what a button does or what's happening to someone's money.

- ✅ (name field error, playful, still clear) "Your name should be long enough to beat a goldfish's memory, but short enough to fit in a fortune cookie — somewhere between 1 and 25 characters."
- ❌ A clever headline on a "send bitcoin" confirmation that leaves the user unsure what they're confirming.

> Rule of thumb: the more money/safety/privacy is at stake, the straighter the copy.

---

## 5. Safety & money copy is plain and direct

**Do** state the safe action simply. Short sentences. No ambiguity.

**Don't** soften critical warnings into vague friendliness.

- ✅ "Always money before BTC."
- ✅ "Meeting in person is safer. Don't share your home address unless you fully trust the other person."
- ❌ "It's usually a good idea to maybe be a little careful with online stuff sometimes."

---

## 6. Errors: say what, why, and what next — no blame

**Do** tell the user what happened and give them a way forward.

**Don't** blame them, dump error codes without context, or leave a dead end.

- ✅ "That code didn't work. Double-check it and try again."
- ❌ "Invalid input."
- ✅ "Are you using a VPN? Try disabling it. Still stuck? Reach out to our support."
- ❌ "Error 4xx: request rejected."

---

## 7. Encourage, don't hype

**Do** celebrate progress warmly and proportionally.

**Don't** beg for engagement or use FOMO/urgency tricks.

- ✅ "You're early. Turn on notifications so you don't miss new offers."
- ✅ "Nice job! All set for the meeting."
- ❌ "🔥🔥 DON'T MISS OUT! New offers are flying off the shelf — ACT NOW!"

---

## 8. Keep it tight

**Do** cut filler. Front-load the point. One idea per sentence in UI.

**Don't** pad with "simply", "just", "please note that", "in order to", "at this time".

- ✅ "Import your contacts to see who's already here."
- ❌ "In order to be able to see who is already here, you will simply need to please import your contacts at this time."

---

## 9. Inclusive and grounded

**Do** write for everyone — no assumptions about age, gender, wealth, or tech skill. Reference real, local, human situations.

**Don't** assume the reader is a crypto bro, a coder, or rich. Don't use insider memes that exclude newcomers.

- ✅ "This is how people actually buy bitcoin without KYC."
- ❌ "GM frens, time to stack sats and stay based, NGMI otherwise."

---

## 10. Emoji: sparing and purposeful

**Do** use the occasional emoji to add warmth or signal tone, mostly in onboarding, success, and social.

**Don't** decorate every line, or use emoji in serious money/safety/error copy.

- ✅ "All set for the meeting 🎉"
- ✅ "🚨 Some offers need to be re-encrypted to be visible to others." _(emoji earns its place as an alert)_
- ❌ Strings of emoji, or emoji in a "payment failed" message.

---

## 11. Punctuation & mechanics

- **Sentence case** for everything: titles, buttons, headings. Not Title Case, not ALL CAPS. ("Post your first offer", not "Post Your First Offer".)
- One exception: genuine acronyms stay caps (BTC, KYC, P2P, QR).
- Use the Oxford comma. Use real apostrophes/quotes if the system supports them.
- Avoid exclamation overload — one is plenty, and most lines need none.
- Numbers: write reach/counts as numerals ("You reach 1,200 people").

---

## The 30-second self-check

1. Human, not institutional? (#1)
2. Does it keep the user in control? (#2, #3)
3. Clear to a newcomer, even without the joke? (#4)
4. If it's money/safety — is it plain and direct? (#5)
5. Any forbidden terms? (see [terminology](./terminology-and-glossary.md))
6. Sentence case, tight, minimal emoji? (#8, #10, #11)
