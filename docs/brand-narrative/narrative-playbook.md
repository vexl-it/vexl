# Narrative Playbook

These are the core stories Vexl tells consistently — in PR, content, partnerships, community, and (in spirit) inside the app. They keep us focused, explainable, and credible, in service of the long-term goal: **growing the user base with higher-quality in-app activity.**

## How to use these narratives

- **PR:** wrap a pitch or interview around **one** primary narrative at a time.
- **Content:** repeat the same framing consistently. Don't drift into other topics.
- **Community:** encourage real stories that naturally reinforce these narratives.
- **In-app copy:** you won't lecture users with these narratives, but the _tone_ of your microcopy should quietly reflect them — privacy as control, no gatekeepers, real people.

### The decision filter

Before publishing anything, ask: **Does this strengthen freedom from gatekeepers, normalize privacy as optionality, or show real people using Vexl?** If not, it's noise.

---

## Primary narrative 1 — "Privacy is practical, not extreme."

- **Core idea:** privacy isn't ideology. It's basic optionality in an uncertain world.
- **Angle:** non-KYC as _financial inclusion_, not rebellion.
- **Key message:** optionality.
- **Framing lines:**
  - "You don't need privacy until you suddenly do."
  - "Optionality is freedom."
  - "Non-KYC isn't anti-law — it's anti-single-point-of-failure."
- **Goal:** normalize non-KYC, defuse the "shady" perception, make Vexl feel like the _rational_ choice, not the radical one.

**Copy implications**

- Frame every privacy feature as _the user's choice_ ("unless you choose to", "until you decide").
- Avoid doom and surveillance-panic. Calm and matter-of-fact beats dramatic.
- ✅ "Your number stays private unless you choose to share it."
- ❌ "Escape the surveillance state before they come for your money."

---

## Primary narrative 2 — "Freedom from gatekeepers."

- **Core idea:** access to money and technology shouldn't depend on approval from platforms, corporations, or centralized intermediaries.
- **Angle:** platform dependency (app stores, centralized exchanges) creates single points of failure.
- **Key message:** permissionless access.
- **Framing lines:**
  - "If someone can block you, you're not free."
  - "Bitcoin doesn't need permission. Neither should the apps around it."
  - "Gatekeepers don't protect users — they control them."
- **Goal:** position Vexl as a legitimate _exit_ from platform control; make alternative distribution (e.g. Freedom Store) understandable; frame Vexl as a practical response to censorship risk — not rebellion for its own sake.

**Copy implications**

- Never use approval/permission/gatekeeping language about users (see [do's & don'ts #2](./dos-and-donts.md)).
- Emphasize that control lives on the user's device and in their hands.
- ✅ "No middlemen. No approvals. Just people trading with people."
- ❌ "Once your account is approved, you may begin trading."

---

## Secondary narrative — "Corporations don't build trust, communities do."

- **Core idea:** trust is built between people, not imposed by brands, platforms, or institutions. Real adoption shows up in small, everyday stories — not metrics or hype.
- **Angle:** peer-to-peer Bitcoin works because it's _social_ before it's technical. Ordinary people, local contexts.
- **Key message:** human trust beats institutional trust. _This_ is what Bitcoin looks like when it's actually used.
- **Framing lines:**
  - "People trust people, not platforms."
  - "This is how people actually buy bitcoin without KYC."
  - "No middlemen, no drama — just peers."
  - "Bitcoin adoption doesn't happen online first — it happens locally."
  - "Communities scale trust better than corporations ever will."
- **Goal:** reinforce credibility through real-world usage, improve quality of in-app activity, and make Vexl feel grounded, human, and socially anchored. Humanize Vexl, remove abstract fear around non-KYC usage, and show that normal people use it as a rational choice.

**Copy implications**

- Lean on "friends and friends of friends" and local, human, in-person framing.
- Prefer real stories and concrete situations over abstract claims or stats.
- This narrative is the direct expression of our north star — **sound human, not institutional.**

## Putting it together: the brand message

**Positioning statement:**

> For anyone who wants to buy or sell Bitcoin, Vexl is a social network that enables peer-to-peer transactions without a digital trace — so they can stay KYC-free, following the original idea of Bitcoin.

**Lead tagline:**

- **"Trade bitcoin privately."**

**Supporting taglines:**

- "Bitcoin as it was intended."
- "Take back your freedom to transact."

**Values that should be felt (not listed) in copy:**

- **Privacy** — no KYC, anonymity by default, chats disappear.
- **Inclusivity** — anyone can join. No ID, deposit, or invitation.
- **Reliability** — solid, professional, trustworthy. (It's still about money.)
- **Localness** — real people who can meet in person.
- **Open source & non-profit** — nothing to hide, nothing to extract.
